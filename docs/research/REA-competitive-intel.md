## 1. Target: what "number 1" actually means

There are four separate rankings, and they reward different things:

| Surface | What wins it (Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, 47 experts, 187 factors) [SEEN] |
|---|---|
| **Google Map Pack** (the 3-pack, where calls come from) | GBP signals 32% · Reviews 20% · On-page 15% · Behavioural 9% · Links 8% · Citations 6% |
| **Local organic** (blue links) | On-page 33% · Links 24% · Behavioural 10% · GBP 7% · Citations 7% · Reviews 6% |
| **AI answers** (ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews, Perplexity) | On-page 24% · Reviews 16% · Citations 13% · Links 13% · GBP 12% |
| **"Best of" lists** (MSARCH, ProsGrade, CommunityVotes, etc.) | Google rating × review volume. These lists are themselves a top AI-visibility factor (#1 citation factor for AI search) |

Top individual factors [SEEN, Whitespark 2026]: #1 Map Pack = **primary GBP category**; #3 = **keywords in the GBP business name**; #5 = **open at time of search (hours)**; #7 = **visible address (not a hidden service-area business)**; #1 local organic = **a dedicated page for each service**. For AI search, 3 of the top 5 factors are citation factors, and "mentions are the new link."

[INFERRED] Komo is a service-area business with a hidden address by choice, which costs some Map Pack strength (#7). It has to make that up on category, hours, reviews and service pages.

### Patterns of the #1s [SEEN across benchmarks → INFERRED as a playbook]
1. **The licence number is a headline asset.** It appears in the header or footer and in an FAQ answer. Only 2 of ~14 Windsor sites checked show it.
2. **Review count shown as a number** ("500+", "162 five-star"), not just "5-star rated".
3. **One page per city/area** (Tron ~45, Elexa, Electro Group, Kraun town lists).
4. **FAQ answers written as citable facts**: "Yes. X holds licence #NNNNNNN…". This is exactly what AI engines quote.
5. **B2B paperwork stated up front**: WSIB clearance, certificate of insurance with a dollar amount, permits in our name, Certificate of Acceptance at closeout.
6. **Quantified proof**: first-pass inspection rate, years in business, project numbers.
7. **Named humans**: owner or master electrician by name (95 Electric, Thames Valley, Actus, IES).
8. **Pricing transparency**: 95 Electric's assessment fee, Stack's guaranteed pricing.

## 5. Confidence roster

### Clone to parity [SEEN in competitors]
- Licence number in the header, footer and an FAQ answer (Tron, Kraun, Astron, Actus, Extreme, Hansen)
- Review count as a visible number (95 Electric, Tron, Thames Valley)
- Click-to-call plus a simple quote form with photo upload (95 Electric)
- A dedicated page per service (MCB, Tron; Whitespark #1 local organic factor)
- Named-town service lists (Extreme, New Age, Kraun)

### Improve on [INFERRED]
- MCB's per-service pages → add **voltage class, phase and typical sites** (spec data AI engines can quote)
- Electricians Windsor's AI findability (88/100 from content alone) → the same structure, plus real credentials and reviews
- Advanced's EV "Tesla badge" → a commercial/industrial capability schedule nobody in Windsor publishes
- Tron's GTA city pages → six Windsor-Essex municipality pages with real local detail (utility: ENWIN / Essex Powerlines / Hydro One, local project types). Keep under ~30 pages to avoid doorway-page risk.

### Gaps nobody in Windsor fills [SEEN absence → exploit]
1. **No Windsor contractor targets GCs online.** The GC-tier firms (Mid South, Vollmer, Rorison) have 4–9 reviews and thin sites. Out-of-town ARC is the only one aiming at "commercial electrical contractor Windsor."
2. **No Windsor site publishes a "how GCs should vet an electrical sub" checklist** (the Astron play).
3. **No Windsor site quantifies inspection performance** (Tron's 99% first-pass). Komo can only publish this once it's tracked and true.
4. **Two of the three named competitors (LMH, DMA) have no working website.** Their visibility is GBP plus reviews only, so they are invisible to content-driven AI answers.
5. **NAP inconsistency is common** (Advanced: 3 addresses; DMA: 2), so a clean, consistent entity stands out.

### Threats [SEEN]
- **Review moat**: 95 Electric (~120), LMH (~94–126) and DMA (~100) against Komo's 10. In the Map Pack this is the gap that matters most (reviews are 20% of the weight). It is not won with a website.
- GTA commercial firms building Windsor landing pages (ARC).

## 8. Recommended order of attack (research → action, no website yet)

1. **Confirm licence #7015083** on the ESA contractor lookup (findacontractor.esasafe.com).
2. **Pick one canonical NAP**: one name ("Komo E.G.C Inc."), one phone (647-767-7815), one email (move to a domain email), one domain, and the service-area setup (hidden address) you chose. Fix Google, both Facebook pages (merge into one), Yelp, YellowPages, contractors1000 and the old directories to match.
3. **Google Business Profile**: primary category "Electrician", secondary "Electrical installation service"; add predefined and custom services matching the capability list (600V three-phase, tenant fit-outs, LED retrofits, machine hookups); real hours (#5 factor); photos of real jobs; licence number in the description (no keyword stuffing, which is a suspension-risk factor).
4. **Review engine** (the largest gap: 10 vs ~100–120): ask every completed client, especially GCs and business owners, for a review that names the service and the town. Review *text* now feeds AI answers.
5. **GC-channel citations**: Windsor Construction Association membership (member directory = GC-facing citation), WECA, ConstructConnect, BBB.
6. **"Best of" lists**: get onto MSARCH, ProsGrade, ElectricianGrade and CommunityVotes Windsor (nominations). These are the #1 AI-search citation factor.
7. **Measure before the build**: run claude-seo `/seo local` + `/seo maps` and `geo audit` on Komo and the top 5 from an unrestricted network to fill the [UNKNOWN] rows.
8. **Then** the website, built on the playbook in sections 3 and 5.
